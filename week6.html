Week 6

Customization & Export for 3D Printing

Making it your own and preparing for the printer

Welcome Back!

Your pirate ship is built! Now it's time to make it yours — and get it ready for the 3D printer.

This week, you'll add colors and materials to bring your ship to life. You'll explore the 3D Warehouse to add trees, flags, and other details. Then you'll learn how to export your model as an STL file — the file format that 3D printers understand.

By the end of this lesson, you'll have a customized, print-ready pirate ship.

What You'll Learn This Week

  • Apply materials and colors creatively
  • Use the 3D Warehouse to add models
  • Export your work as an STL file for 3D printing
  • Understand the 3D printing workflow

Let's Begin

10 minutes

1. Warm-Up: Recap Your Pirate Ship

Open your Week 5 pirate ship model. Before we customize it, let's make sure everything is ready.

Quick review:

  1. Orbit around your ship and check all parts are connected
  2. Verify the hull, deck, cabin, portholes, and plank are all present
  3. Check that the model is a Solid Group (right-click → Entity Info)
  4. Fix any remaining gaps or stray lines with the Eraser tool
A clean model is easier to customize and print. Take a minute to tidy up.
Reminder: Save your file with the format week#-studentid-firstname-lastname.
20 minutes

2. Materials & Colors: Customize the Ship

Materials are what make your model look real. A pirate ship can be wood, metal, and cloth — all made from simple color choices.

Step 1: Open the Materials panel

  • Look for the Materials panel on the right side of the screen
  • Click the dropdown to see categories: Colors, Groundcover, Landscaping, Wood, Metal, etc.

Step 2: Paint the hull (wood)

  • Choose a Wood material — dark brown looks classic
  • Click the outer hull surfaces to apply it
  • Try a lighter wood for the inner hull to add contrast

Step 3: Paint the deck

  • Choose a light wood color for the deck
  • Click the deck surface to apply it
  • This makes the deck stand out from the hull

Step 4: Paint the cabin

  • Pick a contrasting wood color for the cabin
  • Try a red or dark brown roof
  • Add a metal door handle or window frame if you like

Step 5: Paint the plank and details

  • Use a lighter wood for the plank
  • Add a metal look to any ship parts like cannons or wheels
Make it your own. Try a black hull for a "Black Pearl" look, or a bright blue for a fantasy ship. There are no wrong colors on a pirate ship!
15 minutes

3. 3D Warehouse: Add Details

The 3D Warehouse is a library of free models you can drop into your project. You can add trees, flags, animals, or even pirates!

Step 1: Open the 3D Warehouse

  • Look for the 3D Warehouse panel on the right side
  • Or go to File → 3D Warehouse → Get Models

Step 2: Search for a model

  • Type what you want in the search bar — like "pirate flag"
  • Browse the results
  • Click on a model to preview it

Step 3: Add it to your model

  • Click Download or Insert
  • The model appears in your SketchUp file
  • Use the Move tool to position it
  • Use the Scale tool (S) to make it the right size

Ideas for your pirate ship:

  • A pirate flag on top of the cabin
  • A ship's wheel on the deck
  • Barrels or crates on the deck
  • A treasure chest
  • A parrot or sea creature
Careful with size: Models from the 3D Warehouse come in all sizes. Use the Scale tool (S) to fit them to your ship. A flag should be small — not bigger than your cabin!
20 minutes

4. Export as STL File

STL is the file format that 3D printers use. It stands for "Standard Triangle Language" — it breaks your model into triangles that the printer can read.

Step 1: Select and group your model

  • Use the Select tool to drag a box around everything
  • Right-click → Group
  • Check that it's a Solid Group in Entity Info

Step 2: Export as STL

  • Go to File → Export → 3D Model
  • In the file type dropdown, select STL
  • Name your file: week6-studentid-firstname-lastname.stl
  • Click Export
  • Save to your Google Drive or downloads folder

Step 3: Check the STL file

  • Locate the file you just saved
  • Check that it ends with .stl
  • Note the file size — usually a few MB
Export options: SketchUp lets you choose the STL format (ASCII or Binary). Binary is smaller and prints faster — use Binary if given the choice.
If it won't export: Make sure your model is a Solid Group. Non-solid models can't be exported as STL.
20 minutes

5. Open STL in a Slicer (Cura or PrusaSlicer)

A slicer is software that prepares your model for the printer. It slices your 3D model into thin layers and creates the instructions the printer follows.

Step 1: Open your slicer

  • Open Cura or PrusaSlicer on your computer
  • If not installed, ask your teacher for the class computer

Step 2: Import your STL

  • Click File → Open File
  • Select your .stl file
  • Your pirate ship appears on the virtual print bed

Step 3: Explore the preview

  • Click the Preview tab
  • Drag the layer slider on the right side
  • Watch as your ship is sliced into hundreds of thin layers
  • Each layer is one pass of the printer

Step 4: Check the estimated print time

  • Look at the bottom of the screen
  • It shows print time and material usage
  • A small pirate ship might take 2–6 hours to print
Play with the slicer. Try rotating your model, scaling it up or down, and see how the print time changes.
15 minutes

6. Discuss: Printability, Wall Thickness, Overhangs

Not every model can be printed. Some designs have features that are too thin, too tall, or too complex. Let's talk about what makes a model printable.

Wall thickness

  • Walls that are too thin (less than 1mm) may break or print poorly
  • Aim for walls at least 1.2mm thick
  • Your portholes should have thick walls around them

Overhangs

  • An overhang is any part that sticks out into empty air
  • Small overhangs are okay — the printer can handle them
  • Big overhangs need supports (extra material printed underneath)
  • The slicer adds supports automatically if needed

Bridges

  • A bridge is a flat surface printed between two supports
  • Short bridges print fine — long bridges may sag

Scale

  • Your ship needs to fit on the printer's bed (usually 20cm × 20cm)
  • Use the slicer to scale down if needed

Class discussion questions:

  1. Where are the overhangs on your pirate ship?
  2. Which parts are the thinnest?
  3. Will the printer need supports? Where?
  4. Does your ship need to be scaled down?
Real engineers think about this too. Designing for 3D printing is a skill — every printable object went through the same thinking you're doing now.

Save your work:

  2. Rename it: week6-studentid-firstname-lastname
  3. Click Save
  4. Also save your STL file with the same name

Key Words to Remember

Term What It Means
STLThe file format that 3D printers use — models are broken into triangles
SlicerSoftware that turns a 3D model into printer instructions by cutting it into layers
LayerOne thin horizontal slice of a 3D model — the printer prints one layer at a time
Wall ThicknessHow thick the walls of a model are — thin walls may break or print poorly
OverhangAny part of a model that sticks out into empty air
SupportExtra material printed under an overhang to hold it up during printing
