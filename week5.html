Week 5

Pirate Ship Playhouse: Cabin, Portholes & Details

Adding details and preparing for 3D printing

Welcome Back!

Your pirate ship is starting to look like a real ship! You've built the hull and the deck. Now it's time to add the details that make it come alive.

This week, you'll add the ship's cabin, cut round portholes into the hull, and add the famous plank. Then you'll check if your model is printable — ready to be turned into a real 3D object.

By the end of this lesson, you'll have a complete Pirate Ship Playhouse model.

What You'll Learn This Week

  • Build the ship's cabin
  • Create portholes using Circle + Push/Pull
  • Add the plank and finalize your model
  • Check if your model is printable

Let's Begin

10 minutes

20 minutes

2. The Ship's Cabin

The cabin is the small house-like structure at the back of the ship. It's where the captain sleeps. The cabin sits on top of the deck and usually has a door and windows.

Step 1: Draw the cabin base

  • Select the Rectangle tool (R)
  • Draw a rectangle on top of the deck near the back of the ship
  • Type 1m, 1.2m — press Enter
  • This is the footprint of the cabin

Step 2: Pull the cabin walls up

  • Select Push/Pull (P)
  • Click the rectangle face
  • Pull up — type 1m — press Enter
  • You now have a box for the cabin

Step 3: Add a roof (optional)

  • Use the Line tool to draw a triangular roof on top
  • Or just Push/Pull a thinner rectangle on top as a flat roof
  • Both styles look good — choose your favorite

Step 4: Add a door

  • Select the Rectangle tool
  • Draw a small rectangle on the front face of the cabin
  • Type 40cm, 80cm — press Enter
  • Select Push/Pull and push the door inward by 5cm
  • This creates a recessed doorway
Where to place the cabin: Most pirate ships have the cabin at the back (the stern). Place it near the back edge of the deck, leaving space in front for the main deck area.
15 minutes

3. The Portholes (Circle + Push/Pull)

Portholes are the round windows on the side of a ship. They let light in and look great on a model. To make them, we use the Circle tool and Push/Pull.

Step 1: Draw a circle on the hull

  • Orbit to see the side of your ship
  • Select the Circle tool (C)
  • Click on the side of the hull where you want a porthole
  • Type 15cm for the radius — press Enter

Step 2: Push the circle inward

  • Select Push/Pull (P)
  • Click the circle face
  • Push it inward slowly — until it breaks through to the other side
  • This creates a round hole — a porthole!

Step 3: Add more portholes

  • Draw 3–5 portholes along the side of the ship
  • Try to space them evenly
  • Use Select tool to check each porthole is the same size

Step 4: Add portholes to the other side

  • Orbit to the other side of the ship
  • Repeat the same steps to add matching portholes
Careful: Don't push the circle too far or it may create a long tunnel. Just push until you see the hole open up on the other side.
Pro tip: If your circle doesn't snap flat on the hull, hold Shift while placing it to lock it to the surface.
15 minutes

4. Add the Plank

Every pirate ship has a plank — a wooden board sticking out from the side where prisoners (or unlucky sailors!) walk.

Step 1: Draw the plank

  • Orbit to the side of your ship
  • Select the Rectangle tool
  • Draw a long, thin rectangle extending outward from the side of the ship
  • Type 1.5m, 30cm — press Enter

Step 2: Give the plank thickness

  • Select Push/Pull
  • Click the rectangle face
  • Pull up slightly — type 5cm — press Enter
  • The plank is now a solid board

Step 3: Position the plank

  • Use Move (M) if you need to adjust the height
  • The plank should stick out from the side of the hull
  • About midway up the hull is a good place
Add ropes (optional): Use the Line tool to draw thin lines from the tip of the plank back up to the deck. This looks like rope supports.
20 minutes

5. Complete Your Pirate Ship

Now it's your turn to finish your pirate ship. Add the cabin, portholes, and plank. Make it your own.

Required features:

  • A cabin on the deck (with at least a door)
  • At least 3 portholes on each side of the hull
  • A plank sticking out from one side
  • All parts connected — no floating pieces

Optional extras (if you have time):

  • Add a flagpole on top of the cabin
  • Add a ship's wheel using a circle
  • Add small crates or barrels on the deck
  • Add a rudder at the back of the ship

Suggested dimensions:

Part Suggested Size
Cabin base1m × 1.2m
Cabin height1m
Cabin door40cm × 80cm
Porthole radius15cm
Plank length1.5m
Plank width30cm
Plank thickness5cm
Save every few minutes. This is the most detailed lesson so far. Don't lose your work.
20 minutes

6. Solid Check + Fix Common Issues

Before we can 3D print your pirate ship, it needs to be a solid group — a completely closed object with no gaps or holes.

What is a solid group?

  • Every edge is shared by exactly 2 faces
  • No gaps, holes, or open faces
  • Nothing floating separately
  • No inside-out faces

How to check your model:

  • Select your entire model (drag a box around it with the Select tool)
  • Right-click → Group
  • Look at the Entity Info panel on the right
  • It should say "Solid Group"

Common problems and how to fix them:

Problem How to Fix
Stray lines inside the model Use Eraser tool to remove them
Gap in the geometry Use Line tool to close it — trace along the missing edge
Inside-out faces (dark gray/blue) Right-click the face → Reverse Faces
Two overlapping objects Use Intersect Faces then delete the parts inside
Extra hidden geometry View → Hidden Geometry → delete what you don't need

Save your work:

  1. Click the model name at the top
  2. Rename it: week5-studentid-firstname-lastname
  3. Click Save
  4. Verify it appears in your Google Drive
Don't stress if it's not perfect. Solid geometry is the hardest part of 3D modeling. If your model isn't solid yet, that's okay — we'll keep working on it in Week 6 when we prepare for printing.
